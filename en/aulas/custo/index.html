Lesson 1 · 20 min

How much it costs to produce the result

The vendor's dashboard says each Hora conversation costs R$ 0.31. That sounds cheap, and it's the wrong number to decide with. The manager needs to know what each request that reaches billing correctly costs: the invoice, the tools, the hours of whoever reviews, and the rework. In this lesson you do that math for Hora and for one of your own processes.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Why cost per call is misleading
  2. 2Choose the denominator: what counts as a completed task
  3. 3Add up the six lines of a result's cost
  4. 4Why the cheap model that makes mistakes ends up expensive

Goal: calculate the cost per completed task of a process, adding up AI, tools, human time and rework, instead of looking at the cost per call.

  1. 1/6

    Invoice

    The model charges per token, the pieces of text it reads and writes, with a price per million tokens, split into input and output. At Horizonte, the month's invoice came to R$ 1,100 for 3,600 conversations: the R$ 0.31 per conversation on the dashboard. That is the cost of one attempt, not of a result.

  2. 2/6

    Completed task

    Before dividing, write down what counts as done. At Hora: an order that reached fulfillment and was invoiced. There were 680 in the month. The conversations that did not become an order go into the cost and stay out of the denominator. If an answered question is also a result, it becomes another task, with its own calculation.

  3. 3/6

    Tools

    The WhatsApp platform and the order app cost R$ 400 a month. Rafael, from the outsourced IT, spends 6 hours a month tuning Hora, at R$ 90 an hour: R$ 540. Neither of these lines shows up on the model dashboard.

  4. 4/6

    Human time

    Patrícia spends 9 hours a month approving exceptions (R$ 80 an hour: R$ 720). Juliana takes over the conversations Hora can't resolve: 30 hours at R$ 45, R$ 1,350. The agent that "does it on its own" almost always has someone behind it, and that hour costs money.

  5. 5/6

    Rework

    Fulfillment corrected 54 orders in the month (wrong item, gallon quantity instead of box), 12 minutes each: 10.8 hours at R$ 40, R$ 432. Rework is the line that changes most when you switch models, and that is why it drives the choice in lesson 2.

  6. 6/6

    R$ 6.68 per completed order

    Adding it all up, the month cost R$ 4,542 for 680 orders: R$ 6.68 per order. The model invoice is only 24% of that. The number the dashboard shows (R$ 0.31) is real, but it answers a different question.

Horizonte Distribuidora, a fictional study company: all values are fictional. The calculation follows the "total process cost" indicator from the Gestoria method (INEMA).

02 · How to do the calculation

Four steps. The method belongs to the manager, not the programmer: you need a spreadsheet and a week of notes.

1. Choose the denominator

Write in one sentence what counts as a result: "invoiced order", "proposal accepted by the customer", "ticket closed without reopening within 7 days". Count only what got there, in the same period as the invoice.

Attempts, failures and abandoned conversations go in the numerator, never the denominator. This is how the cost of getting it wrong shows up in the math, instead of disappearing into the average.

At HorizonteHora's completed task: an order that reached picking and was invoiced. October: 680. The remaining 2,920 conversations (questions, quotes, drop-offs) are on the bill, but not in the denominator.

2. Add up the whole cost

Model, tools, maintenance, human review, exceptions taken on by people, and rework. For hours, use the hourly cost with payroll charges, which finance can tell you, and log the time for a week instead of guessing.

Separate what recurs from what was spent once (the setup). The initial investment goes into the ROI in lesson 7, not into each month's cost.

At Horizonte (fictional)R$ 1,100 model + R$ 400 tools + R$ 540 maintenance + R$ 720 Patrícia + R$ 1,350 Juliana + R$ 432 rework = R$ 4,542 for the month.

3. The cheap one that gets it wrong

Rafael tested a model at half the price. The bill would drop to R$ 550, but picking started correcting 16% of the orders and Juliana took on 45 hours a month. The cost per order went up from R$ 6.68 to R$ 7.51.

The AI Management area sums it up: a cheap agent that gets it wrong and needs two tries cost double the price list. It's a manager's math.

Test math (fictional)R$ 550 + 400 + 540 + 720 + 2,025 (Juliana) + 872 (rework) = R$ 5,107 ÷ 680 = R$ 7.51 per order.

4. Prices change; the math stays

The price per token drops with each generation. Example from the October overview: Anthropic announced Opus 5.5 at US$ 4 per million input tokens and US$ 20 for output, 40% less than Opus 5 (vendor announcement, 09/22/2026).

At Hora, the model is only 24% of the cost of an order. Cutting the model price in half moves little; cutting rework in half moves more. Always check the vendor's price on the day you do the math.

RuleA model price table gets old within weeks. The cost per completed task math works for any model, from any vendor.

  1. Denominator without a sentence. "Interactions" is not a result: it includes people who only asked the price. Write down what done means.
  2. Human hours left out of the math. "Juliana already gets a salary" doesn't zero out the cost: her hour could go to something else.
  3. Forgotten rework. Ask whoever receives the work (picking, finance) how many cases come back.
  4. Different periods. A bill from one month with orders from another gives a meaningless number.
  5. Double-check: add up the lines again and divide by the denominator. Hora's result must come out to R$ 6.68.

04 · Practice

Hora's math to practice on; your own process's math for real.

1 · Hora's math

20 min

Open the Hora dashboard in the lab and check one week's volume. Copy the template and fill in October's math with the values from the diagram. Then redo the math for the cheap model Rafael tested (bill R$ 550, Juliana 45 h, 16% of orders corrected) and write, in two sentences, which of the two you would keep and why.

Done when: the math has the six lines, the denominator written in one sentence, the result of R$ 6.68 per order, the cheap version at R$ 7.51, and your decision justified by cost per order, not by the model's price.

2 · Your process's math

40 min + 1 week

Pick a process of yours that already uses AI (or one you want to automate). Write the completed task sentence. For one week, ask whoever reviews, corrects or takes over the cases to log their time. At the end, fill in the template and run the hidden cost prompt.

Done when: the math has the denominator in one sentence, the six lines with the source of each number, the hours measured over at least 5 business days, the cost per completed task and the model's share of the total, as a percentage.

Lab volume: Hora dashboard (about 900 conversations and 170 orders per week in October). Amounts in reais: fictional, from this lesson.
