Lesson 6 · 20 min

Vendor and lock-in

AI tools get discontinued, change prices and change rules within months. Whoever finds out on the day of the notice pays more. Lock-in is being stuck with a vendor because leaving costs too much. In this lesson you separate what belongs to the company from what belongs to the vendor, write the exit triggers, and build the switch plan before you need it.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1What belongs to the company and must live outside the platform
  2. 2The signs that you're getting stuck
  3. 3Exit triggers written before the notice
  4. 4Switch plan with cost, owner, deadline and a rehearsal

Goal: plan the switch of a model or tool before you need to: what is yours, what changes, how much it costs, who does it and how long it takes.

02 · How not to get stuck

Four steps. The plan is cheap to write and expensive to improvise.

1. Take what's yours off the platform

Export to a company file everything you wrote and measured. Text and spreadsheets work in any tool; configuration in a proprietary format only works in that one.

Also check that the history (conversations, action logs) comes out in a format you can read outside the platform. History that won't export is the most common form of lock-in.

At Horizonte (fictional)Hora's constitution only existed typed into the WhatsApp platform's dashboard. Now it's in the company file, version 2.0, with Patrícia as its owner; the dashboard gets a copy.

2. Read the signals

Signs you're getting stuck: history that won't export, configuration that only works there, a price that grows with use without a ceiling, a discount in exchange for a long contract, a process depending on a feature still in testing.

A product gets discontinued. OpenAI says the Agent Builder and the AgentKit Evals will be shut down on 11/30/2026, with migration to the Agents SDK or Workspace Agents (vendor announcement). Anyone who built an agent there has a deadline to redo it.

ReadingIt's not one vendor's problem: it's the pace of the market. INEMA's October overview records products being discontinued and rules changing within months.

3. Write the triggers

A good trigger is verifiable: "price goes up more than 30%", "discontinuation notice", "the terms start to allow training on our data", "more than 1 critical error on the 20 cases after a version change".

A trigger may have already fired without anyone noticing. Review the vendors in the lesson 5 inventory with the list in hand.

At HorizonteThe free daily summary service (F10) already has its trigger fired: no contract, and terms that allow training on the conversations. It goes straight into the switch plan.

4. Plan with cost and rehearsal

For each key vendor: what changes in the switch, how much it costs, who does it, the timeline, and how operations keep running in the meantime. During the switch, the affected actions go back to yellow and the test cases run again (G3 rule).

Rehearse once a year: run the 20 cases on the alternative, without switching anything. A plan that has never been rehearsed tends to forget the history, the credentials, and who approves.

Cost of the switch (fictional)Rafael 16 h × R$ 90 = R$ 1,440; Patrícia approving everything in yellow for 2 weeks, 5 extra hours × R$ 80 = R$ 400. Estimated total: R$ 1,840 and 3 weeks.

2. Which data or history does not leave in an open format?
3. Which exit trigger is missing or has no number?
4. Did the cost of the switch forget anything (credentials, history,

Simulation: illustrative example

Exit plan · Hora's model vendor · Horizonte, fictional study company · owner: Patrícia · technical lead: Rafael · approves: Carlos

  • Ours, outside the platform: constitution v2.0, context package v3, matrix and contracts v1, the 20 cases (folder "testes-hora"). Conversation history: export to a spreadsheet, once a month.
  • Theirs: the mid-tier model and its version; the price, checked on the website on 10/15.
  • Triggers: discontinuation; price +30%; cost per request above R$ 8; data terms of use changing; more than 1 critical error in the 20 cases after a version change.
  • Alternative: another mid-tier configuration, from another vendor · trial run on 10/16: 15 correct, 4 acceptable, 1 incorrect, 0 critical.
  • Switch: Rafael reconfigures and tests (16 h, R$ 1,440); Patrícia approves everything in yellow for 2 weeks (5 extra hours, R$ 400). Estimated total R$ 1,840, 3 weeks.
  • Next trial run: October 2027.
  1. "Everything is on the platform." Then nothing is yours. Export it and keep it in the company archive.
  2. Trigger with no number. "If it gets expensive" never fires; "+30%" does.
  3. Switch cost that is only technical. Forgetting the weeks of manual approval and the team training.
  4. Never rehearsed. The first test of the alternative happens on the day the current one goes down.
  5. Check: each item under "what is ours" has a folder path; each trigger has a number or an event; the switch has an owner, hours, R$ and timeline on each line.

04 · Practice

Hora's model vendor plan to practice on; the plan for one of your own vendors for real.

1 · Hora's plan

30 min

Fill in the exit plan for the vendor of Hora's model, using the pieces from lessons 2 to 4 as "what is ours". Then write one extra line for the daily summary service: which trigger has already fired and what the first step of the switch is. Run the prompt and answer each point.

Done when: the plan has at least 5 items under "what is ours" with the folder for each, at least 4 triggers with a number or an event, the alternative with a rehearsal date, the switch table with owner, hours, R$ and timeline on each line, and the daily summary line with the trigger that fired.

2 · One of your vendors

45 min

Choose the AI vendor your process depends on most. Find out with IT what it can export and what it cannot. Write the plan, set the date of the first rehearsal, and take the triggers to whoever approves the purchase.

Done when: the plan is saved with what is yours outside the platform (or the list of what still needs to be exported, with a deadline), the triggers with numbers, the estimated cost of the switch with owner and timeline, and the rehearsal date on the calendar.

Lab pieces: Hora's configuration and customer service summary (F10).
