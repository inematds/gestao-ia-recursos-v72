Lesson 7 · 20 min

Compute budget and ROI

"Look how it's climbing! The agent is paying for itself." Carlos said this while looking at a dashboard that started counting the same day Hora came on board. Nobody measured the before. In this lesson you build the monthly budget with a cap and the estimated ROI in three scenarios, and you practice the conversation where the owner asks for a savings number and you answer without making promises.

02 · How to build and defend the numbers

Four steps. At the end, the whole Horizonte calculation, so you can check it line by line.

1. Budget with a cap

Model cost is variable: it rises with volume, with long conversations and with the top-tier model. That is why it has a monthly cap and an alert before the cap, agreed with IT and, if possible, set up with the provider.

Also set a limit per task: number of attempts and the maximum cost of one conversation. If it goes over, the agent stops and calls a person. Human review is also a governance cost, and it grows: Gartner forecasts worldwide spending on AI governance rising from US$ 492 million in 2026 to more than US$ 1 billion in 2030 (forecast, 02/17/2026).

At Horizonte (fictional)Model: average R$ 1,100, cap R$ 1,400, alert at R$ 1,120 (80%). Per conversation: up to 2 attempts and R$ 1.50; above that, the conversation goes to Juliana.

2. Baseline as a range

Without the "before," any savings number is a guess. Rebuild the baseline with the people who did the work: how long did a WhatsApp order take before Hora? Ask for the shortest and longest reasonable time, not an average.

Write down where the range came from and start measuring now, over a comparable period. A month from now, part of the uncertainty becomes a measurement.

At Horizonte (fictional)Juliana and the salespeople: 8 to 12 minutes per order, most likely 10. With 680 orders and R$ 45 an hour, the human cost before is between R$ 4,080 and R$ 6,120.

3. Three scenarios

Human cost after: Patrícia R$ 720 + Juliana R$ 1,350 + rework R$ 432 = R$ 2,502. Potential benefit: R$ 1,578, R$ 2,598 or R$ 3,618. Minus the month's cost (R$ 2,040): −R$ 462, +R$ 558 or +R$ 1,578.

Estimated ROI: −23%, +27% and +77%. The R$ 3,600 setup cost pays back in about 6.5 months in the likely scenario and never in the pessimistic one. This gets written down, along with the numbers.

ReadingThe most likely result is positive and small; the pessimistic one is negative. The honest sentence is "it probably pays for itself, and we don't know yet," not "it is paying for itself."

4. Capacity is not savings

In the likely scenario, Hora frees up about 63 hours of people's time per month. That is capacity. It only becomes savings if the company cuts an expense or uses the hours on something with a result you can measure.

That is why the selection report separates "potential benefit" from "realized savings" and leaves the latter blank until there is evidence. A simulation is not presented as revenue or as savings.

At HorizonteJuliana's hours went to registering new customers and after-sales. Realized savings: not yet observed. Patrícia tracks for a month where the hours went before any other decision.

  1. A single number. "27% ROI" without the range hides that the pessimistic case is negative.
  2. Capacity added up as savings. Hours freed are not money in the bank.
  3. Investment mixed in. Adding the setup cost to the month drags the ROI down; forgetting it inflates the return.
  4. Baseline from memory, unstated. You can use an estimate, but written as an estimate, with who gave it.
  5. Check: before − 2,502 = benefit; benefit − 2,040 = net; net ÷ 2,040 = ROI. All three Horizonte scenarios have to add up.

    05 · Practice

    Horizonte's budget to train on, yours for real, and the conversation with the owner to rehearse.

    1 · Horizonte's budget

    25 min

    Fill in the template with Horizonte's numbers, line by line, marking what is measured and what is estimated. Check the three columns against the formula. Run the skeptical CFO prompt and write three uncertainties and the decision.

    Done when: the document has the monthly cost with a cap and an alert, the investment separate, the baseline as a range with its source, the three scenarios adding up (−R$ 462, +R$ 558, +R$ 1,578), "realized savings: not yet observed", three written uncertainties, and the date to redo it.

    2 · Your process's budget

    45 min

    Use the math from lesson 1 for your process. Build the budget with a cap, estimate the baseline as a range with the person who did the work, calculate the three scenarios, and write the decision. Show it to someone in finance before taking it to the owner.

    Done when: your document has a cap and an alert for the model, a limit per task, a separate investment, the three scenarios with the formula checked, at least three uncertainties, the freed capacity with a destination, and the date when the estimate becomes measured.
