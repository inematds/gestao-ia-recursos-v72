Lesson 8 · 20 min

Project: the resource plan + What changed

The owner won't read seven documents. They'll read one page: which model, which knowledge, which tools, how much it costs per month and how to get out if the vendor changes. The project is the area's resource plan, with the pieces from lessons 1 to 7 behind it, approved by a rubric and signed by someone who didn't write it. At the end, what changed over the quarter.

02 · From draft to signature

Four steps. The Horizonte plan is practice; the one for your area is what counts.

1. One page

The page summarizes; the pieces prove it. Each block has two or three lines and points to the lesson document that backs it up, in the right version.

Mark what is measured and what is estimated. A plan that mixes the two without saying so is the first to be challenged in the meeting.

At Horizonte"Resource plan · sales · v1 · Oct/2026": one page in front, and behind it the model report, the package, the contracts, the inventory, the exit plan and the budget.

2. Run it through the rubric

The rubric adds up to 100 points across six blocks. It passes with 75 or more and all six mandatory items checked. One missing mandatory item fails the plan, even with 95 points.

The mandatory items prevent the big damage: cost per task with human time, a model chosen by cases, a document with an owner, nothing irreversible within the agent's reach, a spending cap, and ROI in scenarios with no promised savings.

Panel testRun the panel prompt (template next to it). It points out gaps; the score is decided by you and by whoever signs.

3. Who confirms, who signs

Whoever built the plan does not approve it. IT confirms in writing that the guardrails exist in the tool and that the cap is set at the vendor, when possible.

Whoever signs is whoever answers for the budget. They read the page, check the mandatory items, and ask about anything they didn't understand, preferably about the pessimistic scenario.

At HorizontePatrícia builds. Rafael confirms the guardrails for the order app and the email, and the R$ 1,400 cap. Carlos reads, asks about the −R$ 462, and signs.

4. Review date

The plan goes out with a review date: every quarter, together with the shadow AI survey, or sooner if an exit trigger fires or the cost goes over the cap.

At the review, the estimated becomes measured: the baseline, the realized savings, the real cost for the month. That is how ROI stops being a range.

RuleIf the model, the vendor, or the price changed: redo the calculation from lesson 1 and the 20 cases from lesson 2 before touching the budget.

04 · What changed · October 2026

Quarterly panel, replaced at each course review. Each item has a date, a source, and the type of claim. Prices change within weeks: always check the vendor's price on the day. The method in lessons 1 to 7 does not depend on any of them.

1. Prices this quarter

Anthropic: Opus 5.5 at US$ 4/20, 40% less than Opus 5 (09/22/2026); Sonnet 5.5 at the same price as Sonnet 5, US$ 2/10 (09/28/2026); Fable 5.1 about 25% cheaper per task (09/01/2026). xAI: Grok 4.7 at US$ 2/6 (09/21/2026). Sources: vendor announcements and documentation, and trade press.

The INEMA overview reads the quarter this way: the price per task drops 40% to 50% with each generation. It is a reading of the announcements, not a measurement.

For the planFalling prices don't change the math: they change one number in it. Redo the model line with today's price and see how much it moves the cost per completed task.

2. How agents are billed

OpenAI: Workspace Agents, which replace Custom GPTs, are now billed per token (07/06/2026). GPT-6 reached all plans in tiers: Luna on the free plan, Sol on paid plans, Astra on the Pro, Business and Enterprise plans (10/07–08/2026).

Anthropic: Managed Agents, agents that work on their own for hours, are moving toward billing per agent hour, on top of tokens. Sources: release notes and vendor pages.

ReadingPlan tier and billing method change the budget more than the list price does. The monthly cap and the per-task limit from lesson 7 still apply to any billing method.

3. Open and local

Qwen3.8-27B, an open model, runs on a laptop (CNBC, 08/17/2026). For a small or midsize business, open, run locally means a fixed cost and data kept in-house, with your own maintenance and licenses that differ between models.

Provenance matters too: on 09/09/2026, the NSA, CISA and FBI accused DeepSeek, Alibaba, Moonshot and others of "distillation at scale" of American models (The Register). It is a government accusation, not a court decision.

For the selection reportOpen, run locally goes into the recorded alternative with three questions: who maintains it, what license, where it came from.

4. Being retired and governance

OpenAI: Agent Builder and the AgentKit Evals will be shut down on 11/30/2026, with migration to the Agents SDK or Workspace Agents. Gartner: worldwide spending on AI governance of US$ 492 million in 2026, rising to more than US$ 1 billion in 2030 (forecast, 02/17/2026).

A consulting report and a vendor announcement sell what they describe. Use them as a map, never as proof of your case: the proof is your cost-per-completed-task calculation and your 20 cases.

Next review of this panelJanuary 2027, with the following quarter's overview. What is method (lessons 1 to 7) doesn't change; what is price, product and number does.
